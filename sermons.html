#32 November 15, 2026

Self-Sufficient or God-Dependent? (Dependency, Pt 1)

Pastor Matt Scully, Trinity Church
First of a new two-part series on dependency. Recorded 11:19 AM Central, ran 47 minutes. “It’s only going to be a two-parter…about what we have our dependency on.” He opened by telling worship leaders David and Melody that their songs and the word he had been given that morning “just really are partnered with each other.” Theme verse: Prov 3:5–6, read in NASB 1995 — trust in the Lord with all your heart, do not lean on your own understanding, acknowledge Him in all your ways. Big idea: we try to live self-sufficient instead of God-dependent, and self-sufficiency opens three doors: pride, overconfidence, and manipulation. Closing line: “Walk in the confidence that comes with God-dependency, instead of the fear that comes from self-sufficiency.” Benediction: “And I love you. Be blessed.” The problem — and its root. The culture’s idioms: self-made man or woman, DIY, “if you want a job done right, do it yourself.” Often we say that because we relied on someone, they failed us, and we decided it would never happen again. He told on himself as a Gen Xer — house key, no cell phone, home by sundown — and confessed, with his parents in the room, that as a boy he reset his watch when he was running late so it would say he was on time (his mom said it was the first she had heard of it). Americans can usually just go buy what they need, so it is easy to put our dependency on our own ability to provide. His diagnosis: self-sufficiency is often fear-based — “If I am dependent on anything, then I am vulnerable” — and it makes us “the king or the little god of our lives.” 1 — Pride: Nebuchadnezzar (Dan 4:28–37). Read in full, essentially word for word NASB 1995. Babylon, the empire that carried off Daniel, Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego; the Hanging Gardens, one of the Seven Wonders. Twelve months after Daniel interpreted the dream, the king on his roof: “Is this not Babylon the great, which I myself have built…by the might of my power?” “We may not have said it, but we thought it” — the mental trophy shelf. Driven out to eat grass like cattle until he recognized that the Most High rules; then his reason returned, and he praised the King of heaven who “is able to humble those who walk in pride.” Aside: the 2012 “you didn’t build that” campaign remark — whatever one made of the politics, he said there was a lot of truth in it, because every one of us has had help, and “we are not able to accomplish anything apart from God’s grace.” The question he left with the room: if God removed Himself from the equation of life, what would happen? “He’s God; we’re not, and it’s okay.” 2 — Overconfidence: Joshua at Ai. Joshua had seen the Red Sea, the manna and quail, water from the rock, the Jordan. At Jericho he asked God what to do and obeyed the marching orders. Then “this little bitty Podunk town called Ai” — “we got Ai” — and he lost to a much weaker opponent. Joshua went to God, God laid out what had happened, Joshua repented, and he took Ai. The line of the point: “So many times we come up with a plan and we ask God to bless our plan instead of asking God what His plan was.” Then Babel (Gen 11): “I am God. You are a man. Your dependency is upon me, not vice versa…And it is a glorious thing to be dependent upon Him.” 3 — Manipulation: Jacob (Gen 25–32) — where he spent the most time. “Manipulation is where we are scheming to get our way” — with people, and even with God. Gen 25:23 read: two nations in the womb, and the older shall serve the younger. God had already said it would be Jacob’s — “It doesn’t say that they allowed the Lord to set it up…They immediately rolled their sleeves up and manipulated everything to make it happen.” Told as one story: the stew for the birthright (Esau back from “opening morning of deer season”), the goat skins for the blessing, Laban out-manipulating the manipulator with Leah, then the rods and the flocks. “A bowl of soup to get a birthright…some goat skins to get a blessing…the rods to become prosperous.” Then word that Esau was coming with four hundred men, and Jacob “slides right back into manipulation,” arranging his household in front of him and keeping Rachel at the back. Finally he met his match: in the night a man — a messenger of the Lord — wrestled with him and put his hip out of joint, and Jacob “finally has the right answer” (Gen 32:26): “I will not let go unless you bless me.” Applied: “The secret for you is not going to be your ability to manipulate your way to get what you want…how can I hold onto God through the middle of my wrestling” — and walk away blessed “not because I tricked somebody out of it, but because I held onto God until He blessed me.” He gave the room a moment to pray it: “I won’t let go.” The flip — from self-dependence to God-dependence. The aside: after preaching “at that other church” the week before with five Scriptures, a stranger told him he needed to use more Scripture — so this week, “I’m going to make sure I have 25 verses for you.” Ps 121:1–2 (my help comes from the Lord, who made heaven and earth); Ps 46:1–3 (a very present help in trouble, though the mountains slip into the sea); 2 Cor 3:5 (not adequate in ourselves; our adequacy is from God); 2 Cor 12:9 (My grace is sufficient; power perfected in weakness — “because of His grace, not our talents and our abilities”); 2 Cor 9:8 (all sufficiency in everything, an abundance for every good deed). Illustrated with the boy’s lunch and the twelve baskets left over: when God is the sufficiency He goes past the need to abundance — and he widened it on purpose beyond money: “an abundance of joy, an abundance of love, an abundance of patience…It’s not a prosperity gospel.” The struggle he owned. “We realize I can’t save myself…I can’t make God forgive me” — and then, once we receive it, “Thank you, I’ve got it from here,” trying to earn the rest. Instead: He is King, Lord and Father; “it’s not going to make me have just a perfectly comfortable life. That’s not the goal”; a closed door or bad news can be met with “blessed be the name of the Lord,” and that is where joy and peace come from. Close — Isa 41:10. Back to the root he named at the start: fear. Read in NASB 1995 — do not fear, for I am with you; do not anxiously look about you, for I am your God; I will strengthen you, surely I will help you, surely I will uphold you with My righteous right hand. “So, whatever you see on social media…whatever the latest rumor is,” remember what God said. Doctrinal read — where this landed well. The most important move is the one he made in “the struggle he owned”: he applied God-dependence to sanctification, not only to salvation — exactly Paul’s point in Gal 3:3 (having begun by the Spirit, are you now being perfected by the flesh?). And he kept the promise honest: dependence does not guarantee comfort or the outcome you want. That guardrail keeps 2 Cor 9:8 from drifting into the prosperity reading he explicitly rejected. It also sits well beside entry #30: that message said do the work; this one says ask God first whose plan it is. Faith that acts, but acts on His direction. The Dan 4, Gen 25:23, Gen 32:26, Ps 121, Ps 46, 2 Cor and Isa 41:10 readings were all close to NASB 1995. Logged as sound. ⚠ Verification note — why Ai was lost (Josh 7). Overconfidence is really in the story: the spies came back saying only two or three thousand men were needed (7:3). But the reason God gives Joshua is Achan’s theft of the things under the ban — Israel had sinned (7:1, 11–12) — and the victory comes after Achan’s sin is dealt with (7:16–26; 8:1). The verse that actually says Israel failed to ask the LORD’s counsel is Josh 9:14, the Gibeonite treaty, two chapters later. His application (seek God’s plan, don’t ask Him to bless yours) is well grounded — Josh 9:14 is the better proof text for it. ⚠ Verification note — Jacob and Esau’s approach (Gen 32–33). Three corrections, and one of them strengthens his point. (1) The gifts went ahead in droves with servants (32:13–21), not carried by the wives. (2) The household was arranged in three groups — the maids and their children first, Leah and hers next, Rachel and Joseph last (33:1–2) — and that happened the morning after the wrestling, not before it. The “hoping Esau kills them first” motive is his reading; the text says Jacob split his camp so that one group could escape (32:7–8). (3) Between the fear and the wrestling, Jacob prayed his first humble prayer on record — confessing he was unworthy of all the kindness God had shown him (32:9–12). And after Peniel, 33:3 says Jacob himself went on ahead of them all and bowed to the ground seven times. The man who used to hide behind everyone walked out in front. That is the turning point the sermon was reaching for, and it is in the text. ⚠ Verification note — Jacob’s flocks (Gen 30–31). The peeled-rods scheme is in 30:37–43, but Jacob later tells his wives it was God who took Laban’s livestock and gave them to him, and describes a dream from the angel of God (31:9–12). So Scripture’s own verdict is that the rods were not what worked — which fits this sermon perfectly. ⚠ Verification note — smaller details in the Jacob account. Jacob was married to Rachel a week after Leah and then worked the second seven years (29:27–30), not seven years before marrying her. Isaac said he did not know the day of his death (27:2), and in fact lived on many years (35:28–29). Jacob was alone by the ford of the Jabbok when he wrestled (32:22–24), not in a tent. The text calls the wrestler “a man”; Jacob names the place Peniel because he had seen God face to face (32:30), and Hos 12:4 calls him an angel. Not mentioned, but the center of the scene: his name is changed to Israel (32:28). ⚠ Verification note — the Hanging Gardens. Ancient Greek and Roman writers credit Nebuchadnezzar with them, but no Babylonian text mentions them, archaeologists have not found them, and some scholars think the tradition belongs to Nineveh. What is well attested is the boast itself: Nebuchadnezzar’s own building inscriptions brag about his works in Babylon, which fits Dan 4:30 closely. His point does not depend on the gardens. ⚠ Context note — 2 Cor 9:8. In context the “all sufficiency” is given so the Corinthians can give generously to the Jerusalem collection (9:6–11) — abundance for every good deed. His widening to joy, love and patience is a fair application, and his “not a prosperity gospel” line is right, but if this is ever taught from here, start with the giving context. Minor. Only John’s Gospel mentions the boy with the lunch (John 6:9); “five thousand” counts the men (Matt 14:21). “Novice boy” in the transcript is likely Gen 25:27 (a peaceful man, living in tents). ⚠ Speaker identification. Pastor Matt is not named on the recording. Identified by the closing benediction (the same “Be blessed. I love you” that ends #29 and #30), the reference to preaching at another church the week before (Trinity held Testimony Sunday, entry #31), and his parents being present again as on #28. Confirm if anything here is ever quoted. ⚠ Names unconfirmed. “David and Melody” (worship) are as heard. Cross-reference candidates. Pt 2 of this series — expected Nov 22. Entry #30 — Joshua at Jericho is a case study in both; read together they make one teaching (act in faith, on God’s direction). Entry #29 — reasoning with temptation instead of defeating it; Jacob’s schemes are the same instinct. Entry #31 — Ashley quoted Isa 41:10 the week before. Characters library — Jacob would be a strong future library alongside David and Joseph (Gen 25–35; Hos 12:3–4). Theme 1 End Times — Dan 4 sits in the same book as the Daniel 9 study already logged. Theme 8 Prayer & Intimacy — “I will not let you go” (Gen 32:26) with Ps 121 and Ps 46. Verses to pull in NASB 1995: Prov 3:5–6; Dan 4:28–37; Josh 6:1–20; Josh 7:1–26; Josh 8:1; Josh 9:14; Gen 11:1–9; Gen 25:21–34; Gen 27:1–40; Gen 29:15–30; Gen 30:25–43; Gen 31:9–12; Gen 32:3–32; Gen 33:1–4; Hos 12:3–4; Ps 121:1–2; Ps 46:1–3; 2 Cor 3:5; 2 Cor 12:9; 2 Cor 9:6–11; John 6:9–13; Gal 3:3; Isa 41:10.

Testimony Sunday — The Word of Our Testimony

Testimonies from the congregation, Trinity Church
No sermon this week — four testimonies, interviewed from the platform. Recorded 11:12 AM Central, ran 85 minutes including the closing worship. Opened on Rev 12:11 (they overcame by the blood of the Lamb and the word of their testimony) and Acts 1:8 (you will be My witnesses): “What we do with our story is we tell it, and there is power in the story of what God has done.” The host framed the morning as “inspiring people through information given by lives that have been transformed” — the same information/inspiration/transformation language as entry #30 the week before. After each testimony he asked one follow-up question aimed at someone listening who is carrying the same thing. Announcements. Revelation study at 5:00 that evening (chapter 7); intercessory prayer at 4:00. A note on how this entry is written. These are other people’s life stories, told in a room of people who know them. They are summarized here at the level of what God did, not the details of what was suffered. The full account is on the Plaud recording. 1 — Ashley Anderson (wife of the youth pastor). Read from notes (“I write so that I don’t get off track”). A hard childhood with a God-fearing grandmother who kept her in church — “God was planting seeds in me long before I ever knew.” Deep wounds, including from church leaders she trusted as a teenager, and years of numbing the pain. The turn came after a serious car accident, through the ministry of Eddie Davis: she came to know “not just the God my grandmother took me to church to hear about…but a personal, loving, powerful, merciful God.” Real deliverance and years of sharing it across the country — then, back home, losing her mother (three days after learning she was pregnant) and her brother eight months later. “Though my circumstances changed, my God never did.” Her careful line: “I don’t believe the things that happened to me were necessary for God to have a purpose for my life, but I do believe that He has the power to redeem” them. Closing image: she was a fixer-upper, “but thankfully, our God is the best remodeler.” Follow-up — how do you serve in church leadership when church leaders hurt you? Her grandmother’s saying, “people are gonna people.” She chose to look at God instead of the people — honest that there was a season she stopped talking to Him — and to let Him take ashes and make beauty, and let Him bring justice. 2 — Gabby (high school senior, FCA chapter president, cheerleader). She had shared her story at the FCA Fields of Faith event before more than a thousand students. Born premature and fighting for her life, abandoned by her birth parents, raised by family — her grandpa, then Patti, “love at first sight.” During the COVID lockdown she sank into depression over why she was not enough for her birth parents; she named it as foolishness she does not excuse (Prov 26:11) and said she is four years free of it this November. She was honest that stopping was not the same as coming to Jesus. Anxiety was her turning point: she began praying Phil 4:6–7 over panic attacks. A painful breakup this spring put her on her bathroom floor — “that’s when I realized I wasn’t alone.” God fills the void a father or a boyfriend leaves. She still struggles, down to an AP Biology test the Friday before, and she said so: “I am still inching towards the light at the end of the tunnel.” Follow-up — identity. Counseling helped her see the pattern of looking for her worth in boys. Her counsel: you are not alone, there is someone in this room who would pray with you, and “my identity is the daughter of the highest king.” 3 — Tyree Glover. Raised by his mother, grandmother and aunt (“three pillars”); his father was never there. Church as a child was a routine he slept through. At 12 or 13, when the family stopped going, the influences at school got hold of him, in private, “proving the word of God to be true” (Prov 28:13); seeing friends picked up by their dads opened a wound he buried. Basketball became his purpose — prep school, then junior college in Huntington Beach, California — until it stopped working. One night he prayed for guidance to a God he “genuinely didn’t want anything to do with.” The next night, closing at the car wash, a stranger in the empty lot handed him a small camouflage Bible, said he was supposed to read it every day, pointed him to the guidance section — and when Tyree turned around, the man and the car were gone. “My heart was so hard…seeds cannot even take root.” About a year later, done with basketball, he moved to Antioch, Tennessee, and was living with his girlfriend (Prov 14:12, a way that seems right). One night in May the words dropped into his heart, “You need to get right standing with God” — and she was sitting in the kitchen having the same thought. They bought a Bible at Target. Thinking of the Lord’s Prayer, the word Father stopped him; he opened at random to Ps 68:5, “father of the fatherless,” and wept. 1 Thess 4:3 moved them to separate households (in his words, they had “so much access” and “close proximity” — the language of entry #28). At Thompson Station Church he heard the gospel “with a new set of eyes” and said yes to Christ, and went home that day feeling conviction for sin for the first time. Championships, game winners and medals “will not fulfill you”; Jesus is the living water. Last: God moved him from Huntington Beach to a city named Antioch, where the disciples were first called Christians (Acts 11:26). Follow-up — the father wound. Father figures matter, but they are not a biological father; think back over every time you were taken care of, because there is a Father who has looked after you your whole life, chose you before the foundation of the world, and nothing can separate you from His love. 4 — Kim Campbell. Introduced as “a true warrior in the faith,” with the worship team brought up behind her. Her son Daniel was born with total anomalous pulmonary venous return; after surgery on Easter Sunday he was on heart-lung support, and family organized round-the-clock prayer lines. When his blood pressure fell, nurses had been told to pray over him out loud; the surgeon came back to say he could do it, and Daniel came through — she called it a miracle and a picture of the resurrection. She spoke of hard years in her first marriage, and of meeting Chris in 1995, who embraced her and her daughter — “I call him Dad, and that’s exactly what we needed.” Breast cancer around 2006, her sister Cheryl’s cancer and death, caring for her mother through Alzheimer’s, and her own present diagnosis. Through all of it: “He’s never let me fall…He’s always been there.” Her “village” gets her to appointments and takes the notes. Follow-up — what keeps you getting back up? “I have family that needs me. I don’t want anybody to ever think that God has done this to me. He hasn’t. He’s allowed it to happen.” Her aim is to honor Him in her sickness. Close. Worship: It Is Well with My Soul and Because He Lives, then a short exhortation — whatever the outcome, whatever the testimony, “we can live tomorrow because of the goodness of Christ.” Read on the morning. What makes this worth logging next to the sermons is how honest all four were about the unfinished parts. Gabby said she still struggles; Ashley said there was a season she stopped talking to God; Tyree said things still had to be broken off him after he said yes; Kim said the doctors do not expect the cancer to go away. None of them told it as “and then everything was fine,” and that keeps the testimonies true to Rev 12:11 rather than to a formula. Ashley’s line about suffering not being necessary for God’s purpose but being redeemable by Him, and Kim’s “He hasn’t done this to me; He’s allowed it,” are both careful, biblically defensible statements of God’s sovereignty and goodness together (Gen 50:20; Rom 8:28). Counseling was spoken of plainly as part of God’s help, alongside prayer and Scripture. ⚠ Verification note — Antioch. Tyree placed the Antioch of Acts 11:26 in Asia Minor. That Antioch is Antioch of Syria, on the Orontes River (in modern Turkey’s far south, near the Syrian border), the base for Paul’s missionary journeys. The Antioch in Asia Minor proper is Pisidian Antioch (Acts 13:14), a different city. His point is unaffected. Translation note. Gabby quoted Phil 4:6–7 in a paraphrase-style translation (it reads like the NLT), not NASB 1995. Pull NASB 1995 from e-Sword if this is ever quoted on a study page. ⚠ Transcript quality — Kim’s section. The recording of her testimony is badly garbled (room noise and an unclear mic), so this summary keeps to what came through clearly. Names, dates and medical details in her part should be checked with Kim before anything here is quoted or expanded. ⚠ Names unconfirmed. The host was introduced as “Sam” and is otherwise unnamed on the recording. Eddie Davis, Patti and Thompson Station Church are as heard. Confirm before they appear elsewhere. Entry #32 — Isa 41:10 closes that message; Ashley quoted the same promise here (“uphold me with His righteous right hand”). Theme 3 Judgment & Mercy — God permitting versus causing suffering (Gen 50:20; Rom 8:28; 2 Cor 4:16–18). Theme 8 Prayer & Intimacy — Ps 68:5 and the Father heart of God; the round-the-clock prayer over Daniel. #30 November 1, 2026

Inform to Transform — Information, Inspiration, Perspiration

Pastor Matt Scully, Trinity Church
A standalone message, not a series — and he said so. Recorded 11:24 AM Central (the first Sunday back on standard time), ran 49 minutes. “This is not a series. This could have been a four part series. I’m putting all four parts into one message.” He framed his role for the morning as neither preacher nor teacher but “kind of like a spiritual life coach” — how the Word has to be applied to get us through a season and out of who we were into who God wants us to be. Theme verse: Rom 12:2 — do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind. Big idea, in his own formula near the close: “Information, times inspiration, plus perspiration, equals transformation.” Information and inspiration are both good, “but those two things together are still incomplete.” Before the message — announcements and housekeeping. Opened by thanking Pastor Chase for the new announcement video. Wild West Fall Fest that evening: setup at 2:00 with Pastor Shane’s team, festivities 5:00–7:00, with the community usually arriving 30 minutes to an hour early and possibly over a thousand people; cleanup afterward. Newcomers home group: for the last three months he and Lindsey have hosted a group of people new to the church, which is now ready to launch its own home group; a new cohort starts Wednesday with dinner at their house. A postcard and email had gone out and, as of Friday, zero people had responded — “actually, it’s kind of normal for us as a church” — so he asked for RSVPs to plan food. Voting: Tuesday was the last day to vote. “There’s no such thing as a perfect candidate…no matter what color the banner they wave.” He grounded it in Paul using his Roman citizenship to carry the gospel before Caesar, and asked the room to pray, “Jesus, if you were the one in here pushing the button or pulling the lever, who would you be pushing?” Communion was prepared but deferred because “the Holy Spirit seemed to go in a different direction with worship this morning” — at the end of service if time allowed, otherwise likely next week. Opening prayer gave thanks for Saturday’s rain after a drought, noting that in Scripture people prayed for rain and held festivals when it came, and asked that any drought of the Word in the room would end the same way. 1 — Information (logos). He had the room name where their information comes from: social media (“all you got to do to find out how you’re supposed to believe is jump on TikTok”), Instagram influencers, Facebook (“I had no idea that was happening in this world, only to find out it’s not”), Google and AI. He gave honor to the pastors who came before him who had to work through massive volumes all week for a sermon; he types part of a verse into an app and has an answer in ten seconds. Live illustration: he asked Siri for the weather from the pulpit and it answered aloud — rain, high of 75, low of 64. “Information. That quick.” Also: the household “near tragedy” when the internet went down with a three-year-old who wanted a movie, and Alexa’s shopping list coming back with brownies, cookies and donuts (“the kids have been talking to Alexa again…or maybe it was me”), and a phone full of political texts “just trying to slander somebody else.” Then the turn: church is also information — data downloaded through sermon, prayer, song and the hugs in the aisle. Logos and rhema: logos as the written, informed Word; rhema as the inspired Word applied to my life right now. His example: “the Lord is my shepherd” is the logos; “I’m a lost sheep, and I need to be found by my shepherd” is the rhema. The question for the point: is what you received this morning meant to stay data, or to take you somewhere? 2 — Inspiration. Information should lead to something stirring on the inside that moves you toward action — and he said this is a whole-life principle, not a Sunday one: all week you are surrounded by information; what is it inspiring you to do? He had the room picture someone who had not just informed them but inspired them to grow. His own: mornings he would rather pull the covers back over his head, he looks over at his wife, and thinks of his kids, and gets up to earn the paycheck and provide. Then the warning that carries the sermon: “I’ve watched so many of us…we will sit in church. We’ll shed a tear. We’ll have a goosebump. We’ll feel empowered in the moment.” And then the emotion fades and we go back to life. He put himself in the same seat: “I just happen to have a microphone while I’m doing it.” 3 — Perspiration — the point he spent the most time on. “This is where most people get tripped up…We are inspired, but we’re not always willing to put in the work.” His line for it: “We like to lean on the shovel and pray for a hole.” Then a run of case studies, each framed as faith partnered with action. Noah — given the dimensions, the wood and the stalls, and then “for over a hundred years, he had to swing a hammer.” Abraham — promised descendants beyond the stars and the sand and a blessing to the nations, then told to take the son up the mountain; he believed and climbed, and saw God provide. Joshua — given Jericho and the seven-day march: once a day for six days, seven times on the seventh, then the shout. “Joshua believed, but he marched.” David — told he would be king, then at the battle line volunteering against a nine-foot giant; he partnered faith with action by going down to the creek for five stones and stepping onto the field. Peter — “Lord, if it’s you, call me out,” then stepping out of the boat. Applied: we get information on sin, broken marriages, parenting and addiction; the pain of the consequences inspires us; “where we fail is combining that inspiration with some perspiration.” Celebrate Recovery — he said he wrote it in his notes on purpose. He honored the men and women in the room in Celebrate Recovery as people doing the daily hard work to get free and live in it, “for addiction to be a part of your testimony, not your future.” The self-deprecating one. “I have a lot of information on how to lose weight” and inspiration every time his clothes fit differently — “I swear, my dryer is shrinking all my clothes.” His wife suggests they should probably go work out; “that’s code for you’re getting fat. She said, ‘I didn’t say that.’ I bet that’s what I heard.” The point: “I can. But will I? My want-to has to get more and more.” Relationships — the pastoral center of the message. He gets messages weekly from people whose marriages and family relationships are falling apart; one came this week. “My heart hurts for them. But this is like the tenth time I’ve had this conversation…every time, there’s information which leads to inspiration, but I have yet to see the perspiration.” He passed on counsel from an older pastor (of River of Life, now City Life Church) on marriage counseling — “as little as possible,” said as a joke, and then the real point: “a lot of times it’s, hey pastor, will you pick up the hammer and fix this? Whenever what I really need to do is to hand you the hammer and teach you how to fix it.” He was explicit that this was not meant to shame or condemn anyone. Three more cases — when the battle is your own failure or your own crisis. David after Bathsheba — adultery, the cover-up, Uriah’s death (“now David is a fornicating, adulterous murderer. That’s not a good place to be”); confronted by Nathan, he did not just believe and go back to life — he fell on his face, fasted, and prayed for God to restore the joy of his salvation and renew a right spirit. “He went to war against that thing that had destroyed him.” Jehoshaphat — the allied armies gathered against him; rather than “whatever happens, happens,” he called the nation to fast, and the strategy came: worshippers first, then priests, then the army; by the time the army arrived the enemy had turned on itself. Hezekiah — told by Isaiah to set his house in order because he would die; he turned his face to the wall and wept, “remember all I have done for your name,” and before Isaiah got out of the building he was sent back with fifteen more years. “He didn’t just sit there on the information.” The guardrail he put in deliberately — not works salvation. “We don’t work to get saved. We’re not trying to earn our salvation by this. So do not all of a sudden feel like that I’m saying oh now I’m saved by works.” Instead James 2:18, I will show you my faith by my works, and Matt 5:16, let your light shine so they see your good works and glorify the Father — the glory goes to God because He brings the transformation. 4 — Transformation. Back to Rom 12:2: “The goal for you is not information. The goal for you today is not inspiration. The goal for you today is transformation.” And the target is defined, not left as self-improvement: Rom 8:29, predestined to be conformed to the image of His Son, and 2 Cor 3:18, beholding the glory of the Lord and being transformed into the same image from glory to glory. “I’m not just trying to self-improve. I’m trying to look like Christ…Somebody can see Christ in the way that I live.” He applied it to husbands, wives, parents and children. Callback to the last series: “The last couple of sermons that I preached were about forbidden fruit. You got some great information…great inspiration.
