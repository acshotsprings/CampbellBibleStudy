The First Promise (The Protoevangelium)

Genesis 3:15

The very first promise of a coming Redeemer is spoken not in a temple or to a prophet, but in the middle of the wreckage of the Fall. Adam and Eve have sinned, the serpent has deceived them, and before God pronounces the consequences, He turns to the serpent and makes a promise that will echo all the way to Bethlehem and the cross.

Theologians call it the protoevangelium — literally "the first gospel." It is the earliest announcement of the gospel in all of Scripture: that One would come, born of woman, who would crush the head of the enemy, though He Himself would be wounded in the doing of it.

Prophecy dates are approximate scholarly estimates; fulfillment tied to the life of Christ.
Why this is the "first gospel"
Seed of the woman: A striking phrase — descent is normally traced through the man. Many read this as an early hint of a birth unlike any other.
He shall bruise you on the head: A mortal blow to the serpent — the enemy's ultimate defeat.
You shall bruise him on the heel: A real wound to the Redeemer — long read as pointing to the suffering of the cross.

Questions for our study: What did this promise mean to Adam and Eve in the moment they first heard it? How much could they have understood? And how does the rest of Scripture pick up this thread of "the seed"? We hold it loosely where the text is quiet, and let Scripture interpret Scripture (2 Timothy 3:16–17).
