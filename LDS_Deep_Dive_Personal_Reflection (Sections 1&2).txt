MY CONVICTIONS ON THE LDS SYSTEM: A PERSONAL REFLECTION

Jesus said in John 14:6, "I am the way, and the truth, and the life; no one comes to the Father but through me." He didn't say I am a way. He said I am the way. That's exclusive, and I believe He meant it.

The LDS doctrine of three kingdoms of glory directly contradicts this. They teach that almost everyone — even those who reject Christ — can end up in a glorious kingdom. But Jesus said in Matthew 7:13-14 that the way is narrow and few find it. Few. Not most. Not almost everyone. Few. And He spoke of weeping and gnashing of teeth for those who are judged. The LDS can't reconcile their system with what Jesus actually taught about judgment and the exclusivity of His way.

Christ died on the cross. He didn't do it as one good option among many. He did it because it was the only way to get back to the Father. For any man to justify that you could be good enough on your own because Jesus did what He did — that's calling Jesus a liar. And it does not sit well with me at all. You cannot earn your way to heaven. Man cannot live by bread alone. You'll never be good enough to bypass Jesus to get into heaven. That's not how Scripture works.

Joseph Smith claimed revelations that supersede Scripture itself. He placed his authority above Moses, above the prophets, above Jesus's own words. The historical record shows he was a con man — accused and convicted of fraud, manipulation, polygamy he hid from his own wife. I cannot see him as anything except a deliberate deceiver. Yet the LDS system is built on his claims.

Now, I struggle with this deeply because the people I know who are LDS — Hayden's family, good people I care about — they're sincere. They were born into it, taught it from childhood. They're not trying to deceive anyone. They're deceived. And that terrifies me because they're walking toward eternity on a false foundation, no matter how good they are, no matter how sincere.

I grew up Christian. I was taught that the grass is green and the sky is blue. If I'd been taught the grass was red and the sky was purple, I'd believe that too because that's what I was taught. I understand that. But when I test what I believe against Scripture and history, it holds up. The Bible hasn't been disproven. Archaeology confirms it. Manuscript evidence validates it. Joseph Smith's Book of Mormon has none of that. No archaeological evidence. No historical corroboration. Just claims that can't be verified.

So I'm trying to separate my anger at the doctrine — which is justified — from my love for the people caught in it. The doctrine is demonic deception. But the people are sincere victims of it. I'm wrestling with how to speak truth about the LDS system without contempt for the people in it. That's the hardest line I'm trying to walk right now. Because the Fruits of the Spirit are love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control. And I need gentleness and self-control even when my heart is breaking for people I care about who don't see the truth.
