Prophecy 10 · The Sign Fulfilled

They Shall Call His Name Immanuel

Matthew 1:22-23

Seven hundred years earlier, Isaiah gave a sign: a virgin would be with child, and His name would be Immanuel — God with us. Now Matthew, writing the story of Jesus' birth, stops the narrative to point straight back at that prophecy and say, in effect: this is it. The sign has come true. The Gospel writer himself draws the circle closed.

“Now all this took place to fulfill what was spoken by the Lord through the prophet: ‘BEHOLD, THE VIRGIN SHALL BE WITH CHILD AND SHALL BEAR A SON, AND THEY SHALL CALL HIS NAME IMMANUEL,’ which translated means, ‘GOD WITH US.’”

Seven centuries between Isaiah's sign and Matthew writing, 'this took place to fulfill' it.
Prophecy dates are approximate scholarly estimates; fulfillment tied to the life of Christ.
Why this closes the circle
"To fulfill what was spoken": Matthew names the mechanism outright — prophecy given, prophecy kept.
The small caps are the quotation: Matthew quotes Isaiah 7:14 word-for-word, the way the NASB marks Old Testament citations.
"God with us": The name Immanuel is not just a title — it is the whole story. The One foretold is God, come near.

Questions for our study: Why do you think Matthew paused the birth account to quote Isaiah directly? And what does it stir in you to realize the sign given seven centuries early was pointing to a real child in a real manger? We let the text govern (2 Timothy 3:16–17).
