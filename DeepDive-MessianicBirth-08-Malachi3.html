Prophecy 08 · The Messenger Before Him

A Voice to Prepare the Way

Malachi 3:1

Malachi is the last voice of the Old Testament. After him, four centuries of silence — no prophet speaks until a wilderness preacher appears at the Jordan. And Malachi's final promise is exactly that: before the Lord comes to His temple, God will send a forerunnerforerunnerOne sent ahead to prepare the way for someone greater who follows. Malachi's promised messenger is the forerunner — fulfilled in John the Baptist, who prepared Israel for Messiah (see Matthew 11:10; Mark 1:2). to clear the road ahead of Him. The last word before the silence is a promise of the first word after it.

Why this matters for the birth of Messiah
"I am going to send My messenger": Before Messiah, a forerunner would come — fulfilled in John the Baptist (Matthew 11:10).
"Clear the way before Me": The preparation is God's own doing; the coming of Messiah is announced, not accidental.
"Will suddenly come to His temple": The Lord Himself comes — the messenger only points to the One who follows.
The last word before four centuries of silence
Malachi closes the Old Testament. From here, no prophet speaks for roughly four hundred years — until the forerunner appears. The whole story pauses on a promise, and the next voice heard is the one Malachi foretold.

Questions for our study: Why does God send a messenger ahead rather than simply arriving? What does it tell us that the final Old Testament word is a promise of preparation? We let the text govern (2 Timothy 3:16–17).
