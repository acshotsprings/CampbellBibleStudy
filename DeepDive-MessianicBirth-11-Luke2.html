Prophecy 11 · The Birth

Born This Day in the City of David

Luke 2:10-12

Every promise now comes to rest in a manger. Micah named the town — Bethlehem, the city of David. The prophets named the throne, the sign, the timing, the name. And on one ordinary night, to shepherds keeping watch, an angel says the words the whole Old Testament had been leaning toward: a Savior has been born, in the city of David, and He is Christ the Lord. The waiting is over. The Seed has come.

FulfilledThe angel announces the Savior born in the city of David — Bethlehem, just as Micah foretold.
About seven centuries between Micah naming the town and the night it came true.
Prophecy dates are approximate scholarly estimates; fulfillment tied to the life of Christ.
Every thread ties off here
"The city of David": Bethlehem — exactly the town Micah named seven centuries earlier (Micah 5:2).
"A Savior... Christ the Lord": Not merely a king in David's line, but the Anointed One, the Lord Himself.
"For all the people": The promise made to Abraham — that all families would be blessed — opens wide at the manger.

Questions for our study: Stand with the shepherds for a moment — every prophecy we have walked through folds into this one announcement. Which promise means the most to you as you look into that manger? And why do you think God chose shepherds, of all people, to hear it first? We let the text govern (2 Timothy 3:16–17).
