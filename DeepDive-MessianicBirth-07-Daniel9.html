Prophecy 07 · The Timing of His Coming

Until Messiah the Prince

Daniel 9:25

The prophets had named the lineage, the manner, and the birthplace. Now Daniel is given something remarkable — the timing. An angel tells Daniel that from a specific decree to rebuild Jerusalem, a set span of "weeks" would pass until Messiah the Prince appeared. For the first time, God puts the coming of His Anointed on a clock.

Why this matters for the birth of Messiah
"Until Messiah the Prince": The prophecy points to the arrival of the Anointed One — a datable window, not a vague someday.
"Seven weeks and sixty-two weeks": The "weeks" are widely understood as sevens of years, marking out the span to His coming.
A God who keeps time: The same God who named the town also set the season — His plan unfolds on schedule.
A note: why verse 25 and verse 27 sit so close
It is striking that within a few verses Daniel moves from Messiah's arrival (v.25) toward the far end-times horizon (v.27). That is a hallmark of prophetic foreshorteningprophetic foreshorteningA feature of Hebrew prophecy where two events far apart in time appear side by side, like two mountain peaks seen from a distance with a hidden valley between them. Daniel names Messiah's arrival (v.25) and the far end-times horizon (v.27) close together, though a long age separates them — the age we now live in. — two peaks seen close from a distance, with a long valley between them we could not see until we arrived in it. We keep this page on the first peak — His coming — and save verse 27 for our end-times study. Posture: watch, not worry (Matthew 24:36).

Questions for our study: Which decree starts the clock, and what do the "weeks" measure? And how does this timing prophecy strengthen confidence that God's plan for Messiah was precise, not accidental? We let the text govern (2 Timothy 3:16–17).
